RECORD 02HERITAGE KEPT BY MOUNTAINS

The vault of the Caucasus.

Breathtaking and barely reachable — a challenge for us, and a wall for every invader. For centuries, treasures from all of Georgia were carried up here for safekeeping: the Svans knew how to guard heritage, and the mountains did the rest. We continue what the ancestors did — preserving this heritage in the digital age, with methods of our own.

RECORD 05THE REGION AS A QUEUE OF MISSIONS

5,800 km², one mission at a time.

The region is captured as a queue of planned missions: coverage laid over real relief, terrain-following flight so a ridge gets the same detail as a valley, launch points reachable by mountain road.

RECORD 06OUR OWN APPROACH TO FLIGHT AND SCAN AREAS

A 256-megapixel eye.

The primary sensor shoots 256 megapixels per frame. In mountains where every launch is logistics and risk, resolution is economics: more detail from higher altitude, more region per flying hour.

RECORD 07A FOREST PLACED BY DATA

The forest plants itself.

Real species from the region's elevation belts, captured tree by tree and rebuilt as hyper-real impostors at splat-level quality — grass and fur included — inside a mobile budget. Segmentation masks decide where each species grows: the forest is placed by data, not by hand.

RECORD 08MISSIONS IN, FINISHED LEVELS OUT

From memory card to a place.

A pipeline turns raw missions into finished pieces of the world — automatically. The unit of that world is not a map tile: it's a complete interactive level, delivered for exactly the place you need. A region of this size cannot be built by hand — so we built the factory.

RECORD 09THE SAME PLACE, AGAIN AND AGAIN

An archive with a time axis.

Missions re-fly the same routes, and a single site accumulates dozens of scans — a place you can scrub through time: something rising, something restored, a glacier retreating. Other twins store snapshots. This archive stores the film.
